Ch 01 Real Numbers
Number System ▶

Euclid's division lemma, fundamental theorem of arithmetic, irrational numbers, decimal expansions.

Euclid's Division LemmaFundamental Theorem of ArithmeticHCF & LCM by Prime FactorisationIrrational Numbers ProofDecimal Expansions

🧮 Formulae / Key Expressions

HCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × bEuclid: a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b

📌 Key Points

  • Every composite number can be expressed as a product of primes (uniquely)
  • √2, √3 are irrational — proved by contradiction
  • Terminating decimal ↔ denominator has only 2s and 5s as prime factors
  • HCF × LCM = Product of two numbers

Ch 03 Quadratic Equations
Algebra ▶

Solving quadratic equations by factorisation, completing the square and quadratic formula.

Standard Form ax²+bx+c=0FactorisationCompleting the SquareQuadratic FormulaDiscriminant

🧮 Formulae / Key Expressions

x = [-b ± √(b²-4ac)] / 2aDiscriminant D = b²-4acD>0: 2 real roots, D=0: equal roots, D<0: no real roots

📌 Key Points

  • D > 0 → two distinct real roots
  • D = 0 → two equal roots (one real root)
  • D < 0 → no real roots
  • Always check: can it be factorised before using formula?

Ch 06 Probability
Statistics ▶

Classical probability, compound events, complementary events.

Classical DefinitionSample SpaceFavourable OutcomesComplementary EventsSimple Experiments

🧮 Formulae / Key Expressions

P(E) = Number of favourable outcomes / Total outcomesP(E) + P(Ē) = 10 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

📌 Key Points

  • Probability is always between 0 and 1
  • P(impossible event) = 0
  • P(certain event) = 1
  • For a fair coin: P(H) = P(T) = ½
  • For a die: P(any number) = 1/6

Ch 01 Chemical Reactions & Equations
Chemistry ▶

Types of chemical reactions, balancing equations, oxidation and reduction.

Balancing EquationsCombination ReactionDecomposition ReactionDisplacement ReactionOxidation & Reduction

🧮 Formulae / Key Expressions

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O (Combination)2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (Decomposition)CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (Thermal Decomposition)

📌 Key Points

  • A chemical equation must be balanced (law of conservation of mass)
  • Oxidation = loss of electrons / gain of oxygen
  • Reduction = gain of electrons / loss of oxygen
  • OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain
